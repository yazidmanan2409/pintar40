PINTAR public website · review build 3 · launch wording for PINTAR 4.1

Understand what to teach next.

This build carries the intended launch wording for when PINTAR 4.1 has been accepted by Dr Gladys and her team. Preparing the wording does not mean acceptance has occurred. Every page is noindex and carries a review strip, and nothing has been deployed. Publication needs the accepted scope, public claims that match it, and the owner’s explicit authorisation.

1. What changed in this revision

The story

  • One organising idea: learning objective → evidence → educator judgement → next teaching step. It is drawn once as a thread with four marks, and the same marks label the hero panel and the annotated report, so the sequence is recognisable without being repeated as a slogan.
  • Homepage opening: «Nilai menunjukkan hasil. Pemahaman menentukan langkah berikutnya.» / «A score shows the result. Understanding shows what to teach next.» The lead is one simple sentence; the class panel carries the detail.
  • The class panel is complete: learning objectives with «% of answers correct» from 3 questions and 24 learners, said to be evidence and not yet a judgement of mastery; the hardest questions with counts and the option most chosen; then a separate note, «Written by the educator · example», with a judgement and the next step. «Change since the Initial Mapping» is removed until its support is confirmed. The panel does not suggest PINTAR recommends an intervention.
  • One current product: the availability chips and A/B letters are gone from Features and the homepage. «New in 4.1» is a small tag on three items. Versions and the future direction sit together at the end of Features; the homepage has two sentences and a link instead of the roadmap.

The Features page, redesigned

  • Organised by what people accomplish: learning and teaching, reports and assessment, the Content Studio, accounts and institutions, AI-assisted drafts, then versions and what comes next. An anchor bar replaces the status legend.
  • The reading pattern of Who It’s For: a clear label, a headline at a sensible size, the explanation directly under it, four well-spaced benefit rows at body size, and one focal visual. The small two-column notes and the detached intro paragraphs are gone.
  • Variety within one system: journey on the right; the annotated report on the left; the publication diagram full width with three short notes; the roles diagram on the right; the AI workflow full width in a sunk band under «AI membantu. Pendidik memutuskan.» On phones every section reads headline, text, then visual.
  • Each visual does one job: the learning journey shows the starting point, assigned learning, practice and assessment; the annotated report shows what an educator notices instead of an audience table; one publication diagram covers review, shared use and preserved versions; the roles diagram shows that access comes from the role assigned in each place.

Copy, both languages

  • No internal-review language in public copy: «not part of 4.0», «not promised here», «not an audit report», «ready to show at the time» and the repeated «no dates or promises» are gone. The one future boundary sits in the versions section; the rest is on the checklist below. A check now fails if any of these return.
  • Demonstrations: «a guided demonstration tailored to your role, using sample data».
  • Simplified phrasing: «what can be concluded», «which figures the evidence supports», «mastery by part», «the evidence minimum», «the usual approval» and «removed access» are rewritten.
  • Institutions are no longer a person’s role: Who It’s For now covers four roles and «the institution as a whole»; the homepage lists «foundations and governing bodies».
  • Terms now names Inter and IBM Plex Mono, and explains that AI-generated illustrations are not photographs of PINTAR users or partner institutions.

Images

  • Five images instead of eight, each placed where it explains the situation: the classroom, the staff room, studying at home, a leadership meeting, the school in the morning. Why PINTAR and the demo bands now use the thread and typography instead of a photograph; administrators get the roles diagram.
  • Every image declares its source: AI-generated illustration, licensed photograph, or product screenshot. Generated images carry a visible «Ilustrasi, dibuat dengan AI» label and are never presented as PINTAR users. No product screenshot is used or faked.
  • Drop-in: save an image as images/P1.png etc., run node images.mjs (WebP at 640–2400 px, metadata removed) and node build.mjs.

5. Verified in this build

  • Reading order: on Features, every section’s headline and text come before its visual in the document, and sit above it at 390 px; at 1440 px the compositions alternate. The cycle keeps stages 1–7 then the return, as a serpentine at 1440 px and one column at 390 px.
  • Body-text comfort: introductions and benefit rows are at least 15 px with a line height of at least 1.4 and no wider than 40 em, on every page at 390 and 1440 px.
  • Diagram legibility: no text under 14 px anywhere; no diagram overflows its column at 320, 390, 768, 1024 or 1440 px; contrast is measured on every page.
  • Story and accuracy: the hero headline; the panel’s «sample data», what each percentage measures, the learner count, and percentage correct distinguished from mastery; the educator’s decision set apart and attributed; the thread on Home and Why; no availability chips or A/B letters on Features; versions last; no internal-review phrasing on any page; every image declares its source.
  • Unchanged and still checked: language switching on all 16 pages (header and footer); the contact form (delivery, double submit, failed delivery and a real retry, moderation in both languages without echo, flagged reports, honeypot, rate limit, origin, header injection, no JavaScript); the footer and the two demo placements; 47 moderation cases.
  • Run: node verify/check.mjs. The new checks were proved able to fail by planting each regression: internal-review wording, the educator’s note merged into the report, and a visual placed before its headline.
