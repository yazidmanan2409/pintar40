## The blocker

**The cycle.** On the issuer's side, take `recovery_grant_permit`, or `password_write_permit` the same way:
1. it takes the **account** row `FOR SHARE`;
2. then it inserts into `password_write_permits`. That fires L1-F4's BEFORE INSERT guard
   (`_l1f4_password_write_permits_auth_user_id` → `orang_dianonimkan`), which takes the **person** row `FOR SHARE`.

On the suspender's side, `tangguhkan_akun` or `minta_akun` (CLOSE):
1. it takes the **person** row `FOR UPDATE`;
2. then `akun_kunci` takes the **account** rows `FOR UPDATE`.

So the issuer locks account then person, and the suspender locks person then account. Suppose the suspender takes
the person row between the issuer's step 1 and step 2. The issuer then waits on the person and the suspender waits on
the account: a deadlock. Postgres aborts one of them with `40P01` after `deadlock_timeout`.

**Why it blocks.** No bad state commits, so no permit outlives a lock. But either side may be the one aborted. If it
is the suspension, an administrator's suspension (or a person's own closure) fails because of a recovery or
activation running at that moment. The person being suspended can start that recovery themselves. A security
intervention must not be defeatable by its subject, even rarely and retryably. The page also states the order as "no
cycle", and that statement must be true.

The page's own lock-order note says L1-F4's guard on `password_write_permits` takes the person row, and that "the
suspending act takes that row before step 2, so the order is still person, then account, on both sides". That holds
for the suspender, but not for the issuer, which has already taken the account row before the insert.

**The fix.** Both issuers take the **person** row `FOR SHARE` first, then the account row, then run their check:
person, then account, on both sides.
- In `recovery_grant_permit`: resolve the grant's account to its person, lock that person row, then the account row.
- In `password_write_permit`: the same.

This makes L1-F4's later `FOR SHARE` on the same row a no-op, and it serialises the issuer against the whole
suspending act, not only against `akun_kunci`. Keep the account `FOR SHARE` too (harmless, and in order).

## The four questions

- **And then what?** An account made during a suspension or closure is locked from its first instant. Reinstatement
  or reopening releases it with the person's other accounts.
- **What else did that change?** Every account-creation path, through the one insert; the recovery and activation
  permits around a lock; the lock order between issuers and suspension (the blocker).

- **Can it be reversed, and what survives?** Reinstatement unlocks. A later suspension locks again. A
