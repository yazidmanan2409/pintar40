## First, one fact to establish (blocking for L-75, and possibly a finding in its own right)

**Does Supabase Auth's `user_banned` depend on the password?**
- A closed or suspended account is banned in Auth (`akun_kunci` sets `banned_until`). PINTAR's server maps `user_banned` to the ordinary non-match.
- **But Auth's token endpoint can be called directly with the public anon key.** If Auth answers `user_banned` **before checking the password**, then anyone who knows an email already learns, from Auth itself, that the account is closed or suspended. PINTAR's uniform wording does not prevent that. **That would be an existing state oracle,** and a finding independent of L-75.
- **The test** (local stack, read-only of Auth's behaviour), with three accounts called directly at `/auth/v1/token?grant_type=password`:
  - a banned account with the right password;
  - a banned account with a wrong password;
  - an ordinary account with a wrong password.
- **Record** the status, `error_code`, the body, and the median time over 20 calls each. The same for the card path.

## L-75: the closed account with the CORRECT password

**My decision:** the disclosure is **acceptable for a password account**, under three conditions.
1. **Only after the password is proven,** on the server. If the test shows Auth checks the password before `user_banned`, then `user_banned` itself is that proof.
   - If the test shows Auth answers `user_banned` regardless of the password, the closed state is already public at the API, and L-75 cannot rely on Auth's code. **Stop and report:** the fix then belongs at Auth's layer (for example not banning, and refusing in PINTAR's per-request check), which is a different design.
2. **Nothing a guesser can distinguish on the wrong-password path:**
   - the same status and body as today;
   - the same `identity_attempt` counting;
   - time equalised, since PINTAR already holds a minimum response delay (`jeda`).
   - The new message is returned only on the proven path.
   - **Why the disclosure is safe:** a guesser who has proven the password of an open account already gets the strongest signal of all, a sign-in. So telling a proven closed account "closed" adds nothing beyond what open accounts already give.
3. **Reopening re-verifies, and it must also re-key.** A closed account's password is proven here, then reopened later. Reopening, which is already by request after identity verification, should **require a new password** at first sign-in after reopen. Then a password learnt while the account was closed is worth nothing.

**The learner's card (PIN) sign-in: do NOT disclose.** Keep the ordinary non-match.
- A PIN is low-entropy, and a closed learner's account (closed through the school, `ACCOUNT_LEARNER_VIA_SCHOOL`) gives a guesser no success signal today.
- The disclosure would turn it into a PIN-confirmation signal for an account that can later be reopened.
- **The learner's help is L-76's generic line, and the school.**

## L-76: one line shown to everyone on PIN recovery

**CONFIRMED: it reveals nothing**, provided:
- it is shown on **every** PIN-recovery refusal, whatever the account;
- it is never conditioned on whether a reset happened;
- the response and its timing are otherwise unchanged.

The line («Bila PIN Anda baru diatur ulang oleh sekolah, pulihkan lewat email atau tanyakan kepada admin sekolah.») states a general possibility, not a fact about any account.

## C-9 (Daisy's cause): agreed

**The principle:** a refusal worded to hide a state from strangers may say more **only once the requester has proven what a stranger cannot know**, and only where that proof is strong. A password qualifies; a PIN does not.
