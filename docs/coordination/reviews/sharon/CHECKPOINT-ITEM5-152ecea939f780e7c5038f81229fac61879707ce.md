## What holds now

**Yes. A restore of staging's backup into a scratch target satisfies §5b item 5**, under the five conditions below. A
restore of staging itself is not needed. **Nothing stops, and nothing goes to the owner on this point.**

If any condition cannot be met, or the rehearsal's result differs from staging after the runbook step, that is a
material discrepancy: stop and escalate.

## Why it satisfies the item

Item 5 reads:
> "A hosted restore brings back the accountability tables and the jobs, **or** the native-restore runbook step
> reinstalls them, and the health read is then clean."

It is satisfied by either branch. The second branch is the one that matters, and a scratch target tests it fully.
- `restore-rehearsal.yml` takes **staging's own** logical backup (Supabase's documented CLI procedure) and restores it
  into an empty stack on the runner, by the documented psql procedure.
- It compares table by table: row counts and content hashes, triggers, the pre-request hook, functions, grants and
  policies.
- The backup never leaves the runner, and staging is only read.
- The shared job installer is idempotent ("installing twice leaves exactly one", `20270114110000`). So running the
  runbook step after **any** restore gives the correct jobs, whatever a hosted restore did or did not bring back.

What the scratch target does **not** show is the first branch: what Supabase's own hosted restore brings back by
itself. Item 5 does not require that branch, and finding it out would mean restoring staging, which nobody has
authorized and which nothing here needs. **This limit must be recorded in the verification report as a known limit,
not left out.**

## The five conditions

1. **After the promotion.** The rehearsal restores the promoted schema, not today's.
2. **The comparison covers what these batches add:**
   - `pintar_kunci_tetap_jaga` on `auth.users` and `_l1f2_accounts_terkunci` on `accounts`;
   - L1-F4's guards;
   - `cron.job`'s rows;
   - the accountability tables (`accountability_events`, `accountability_write_failures`).
   - A copy that differs from staging on any of them, after condition 3, is a material discrepancy.
3. **The runbook step, run and run again on the copy.**
   - If `cron.job` did not come back, the shared installer brings it back on the copy, each job exactly once.
   - Run it a second time: still exactly once.
   - Then the health read on the copy is clean.
4. **The `audit_log` rewrite time.**
   - Measure it per statement in the promotion run, or by timing that migration's statement on the restored copy.
   - Record it with staging's `audit_log` row count, so it can be scaled to production's for the maintenance window.
5. **The known limit**, recorded as above.
