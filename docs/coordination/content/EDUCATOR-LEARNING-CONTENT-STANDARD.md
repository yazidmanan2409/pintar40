# Educator learning: audience and pedagogical standard

**Status:** set by Yazid, 30 Sep 2026; the visual and experience rule added the same day (D-069(g)).

**Applies to** all content written for educators learning through PINTAR. It starts with the two-chapter «Strategi Pembelajaran» demo (internal test and demonstration content only).

**Relationship to PINTAR's other rules:**

- Terminology v1 (D-068) and the design rules (`design/PINTAR-DESIGN-SKILL-SPEC.md`) still apply.
- This standard governs who the content speaks to and how it teaches.
- The underlying PINTAR learning engine may be shared with student learning, but the pedagogical treatment must suit an adult professional educator.

> **Do NOT write or present this content as if the learner is a primary-school, secondary-school or university student.**

## Audience

Assume the learner is a practising educator. They already have:

- classroom experience;
- responsibility for learners;
- familiarity with teaching and assessment;
- professional judgement;
- practical problems they meet in real classrooms;
- limited time.

Respect that experience. Do not patronise the educator. Do not over-explain elementary concepts merely to make a lesson look comprehensive.

## Teaching approach

Present concepts through the educator's professional context. Prefer this sequence:

1. concept;
2. why it matters in teaching;
3. a realistic classroom situation;
4. the educator considers what they would do;
5. the principle or explanation;
6. application to their own teaching.

Where appropriate, ask the educator to connect the material with their own experience. For example:

- «Think about a lesson you taught recently…»
- «Consider a class in which several students understood the material quickly while others struggled…»
- «Which approach would you choose in this situation, and why?»

The purpose is not merely to test recall. It is to connect educational theory with professional practice.

## Language

Natural, professional Bahasa Indonesia suited to educators.

The tone is:

- adult;
- respectful;
- clear;
- concise;
- practical;
- intellectually credible;
- warm without becoming casual or childish.

**Avoid:**

- childish encouragement;
- excessive simplification;
- school-child examples where a professional example would serve better;
- cartoonish language;
- gamification;
- unnecessary praise;
- «Hebat!», «Keren!», «Ayo, kamu pasti bisa!»;
- artificial enthusiasm.

PINTAR acknowledges progress quietly and professionally.

## Examples

Examples should mostly come from situations an educator recognises:

- planning a lesson;
- selecting a teaching strategy;
- explaining a difficult concept;
- students with different levels of understanding;
- classroom participation;
- choosing activities;
- assessment;
- interpreting student responses;
- changing strategy when a lesson is not working;
- reflecting after teaching.

Do not use examples designed for children, unless the example is discussed from the **educator's** perspective:

- **Wrong audience:** «Rina has 5 pencils and receives 3 more. How many pencils does she have?»
- **The educator's perspective:** «During a mathematics lesson, several students can perform the calculation correctly but cannot explain why the method works. What should the teacher consider changing in the next learning activity?»

## Assessment standard

Assess professional understanding, not merely memory. Use a mixture of:

1. conceptual understanding;
2. application;
3. classroom scenarios;
4. professional judgement;
5. interpretation;
6. recall, only where genuinely necessary.

Prefer questions such as:

- «Seorang guru menghadapi…»
- «Pada akhir pembelajaran, guru menemukan…»
- «Jika tujuan pembelajaran adalah …, strategi mana yang paling sesuai dan mengapa?»

rather than filling the assessment with «Menurut definisi pada materi…».

Do not make every question a scenario merely for variety. Use the question form that best tests the intended understanding.

All answers and distractors must be plausible and professionally credible. Avoid answer-length bias.

## Adult-learning principle

The educator should often be able to connect new knowledge with existing professional experience. Where it helps, prefer **Explain → Reflect → Apply** over **Explain → Memorise → Test**.

Do not force a reflection exercise into every lesson. Use one where it materially improves understanding.

## The PINTAR experience

The educator gets the same fundamental quality of PINTAR learning that students get:

- clear progression;
- manageable lesson length;
- an obvious next action;
- assessment feedback;
- visible progress;
- the ability to stop and continue later.

The **content** experience must clearly communicate: *"PINTAR understands that I am an educator."* The educator is a learner here, and is not treated as a child.

## Visual and experience rule (Yazid, 30 Sep; D-069(g))

Educator professional learning must look like professional learning. Same PINTAR design DNA; different learner context.

**Do not use:**

- comics, cartoons or mascots;
- childish illustrations, sticker graphics or classroom clip-art;
- childish visual metaphors;
- decorative imagery merely to fill space;
- gamification: XP, streaks, badges for ordinary completion;
- exaggerated celebration, confetti, fake applause (including applause sound).

> **PINTAR acknowledges. PINTAR does not perform.**

**Suitable, where useful:**

- diagrams, frameworks, process flows and conceptual models;
- restrained information graphics;
- professional photography where justified;
- excellent typography and hierarchy.

If a concept does not need a visual, do not add one.

The content must not feel as though it was adapted from an SD learning experience. Where it fits, prefer: concept → why it matters → classroom situation → professional judgement → application.
