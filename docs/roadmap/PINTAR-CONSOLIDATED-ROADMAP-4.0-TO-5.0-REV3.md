2. **Evidence and insight classification.** Store whether each statement is **recorded** (observed), **derived** (reproducible calculation), **inferred** (explicit versioned rule) or **not concluded** (insufficient or unsuitable evidence). Carry evidence references, versions, denominator, exclusions, limitations and sufficiency conditions as applicable. AI may express an approved conclusion. 4. **Governed formal educator evaluation.** See the lifecycle below.
5. **Educator protections before sharing educator-related evidence.** Layer access and audit it, give educators appropriate visibility and ways to correct or respond, separate developmental and formal evaluation records, and restrict unsupported comparisons. Institutional access and record responsibilities depend on policy.
6. **Explainable history.** Use immutable snapshots, as-of reconstruction and versioned templates to reproduce what reports, sealed evaluations and snapshot-rendered certificates said when issued. This retains the internal continuity capability from the old Context Passport proposal.
### How PINTAR Intelligence Works

**Evidence → Measurement → Interpretation → Explanation → Educator Decision**

| Stage | Responsibility and limit |
|---|---|
| Evidence | Collect legitimate records from governed learning and assessment, including revisions, scoring policy, sitting conditions and authorized corrections. Access follows the existing permission boundary. |
| Measurement | Apply documented, versioned rules. Expose denominators and exclusions, check question quality and match competency identity and instrument comparability before combining evidence. |
| Interpretation | Classify direct observations as recorded and reproducible measures as derived. Use explicit rules for inferences, with sufficiency and applicability checks. Use “not concluded” when those checks fail. |
| Explanation | Present the evidence, limitations and relevant context through Quiet Intelligence. AI may help express an approved conclusion within the governed access boundary. |
| Educator Decision | The educator evaluates what the evidence means for teaching and chooses whether to act. A formal assessment of educator performance requires the separate human-authorized lifecycle below. |

**Illustrative journey.** Many learners choose the same incorrect option on a fractions question. Question Intelligence first checks the key, revision, distractor behavior and evidence sufficiency. An ambiguous stem or flawed distractor can explain a low score, so a poorly constructed question cannot automatically establish weak learner understanding. Competency identity connects trustworthy items to the same underlying skill. Comparability determines which distributions can be read together. Performance Distribution shows the spread and the denominator. Quiet Intelligence may explain a supported pattern across eligible items or report that a conclusion about the competency is unwarranted. The teacher decides whether to inspect the question, gather more evidence or revisit the concept.

### Governed Formal Educator Evaluation

The approved architecture is institution-governed and human-finalized. The following lifecycle elaborates the approved seven-step architecture. R-1 determines the institution’s detailed authority and procedures. [S1]

1. The institution establishes an authorized evaluation period and eligible evidence rules.
2. Eligible evidence accumulates with provenance, scope, revisions, participation, exclusions and unusual sitting conditions. Keep the educator’s assessment decisions visible.
3. The period closes under the institution’s policy, defining what evidence was available at that point.
4. PINTAR prepares an evidence-backed draft. It states limitations and uncertainty and withholds unsupported conclusions through the evidence classification.
5. An authorized human assessor reviews the draft and supporting and contrary evidence.
6. The assessor can accept or adjust conclusions and scores under the agreed rubric.
7. Adjustments carry a recorded rationale.
8. Preserve the original draft and the assessor’s decisions with traceable evidence, versioned rules and audited access.
9. The assessor finalizes and seals the evaluation. Explainable history reproduces the sealed record.
10. The educator receives the finalized evaluation.
11. Present supporting evidence, strengths, areas requiring attention and specific recommendations.
12. Apply approved inspection, correction, response, readership and retention rules. R-1 still determines the response window and whether the response joins the sealed record, among other policy choices.

Keep ordinary learning feedback and private developmental work separate from formal evaluation. The educator has appropriate inspection and response rights. Insufficient or incomparable evidence cannot support a formal conclusion. The system creates no automatic educator rankings, comparisons between named educators or automatic disciplinary consequences. The 4.5 foundation supplies traceability and protections that can support 5.0 growth, while institutionally sealed evaluations and educator-led growth records remain subject to their own permissions and purposes.

### The PINTAR Improvement Cycle

**Understand → Choose → Act → Observe → Compare → Reflect → Improve**

A teacher reads a Quiet Intelligence explanation that a group may be struggling with fractions and checks the evidence and its limitations. The teacher chooses to reteach equivalence with visual models and runs the intervention. PINTAR records what happened, which learners were affected, the timing and relevant context. Further learning and assessment evidence accumulates. PINTAR compares outcomes where the instruments, competency coverage and participating groups permit it, and shows relevant exclusions and alternative explanations.

| What can be said | Basis |
|---|---|
| **The intervention was recorded** | A governed record establishes what was done. |
| **Later outcomes were consistent with improvement** | Eligible, comparable observations show a pattern consistent with improvement, with limits stated. |
| **An improvement pattern received stronger support** | Appropriately designed repeated observations address relevant alternative explanations. Claim strength still depends on evidence and design. |

A simple before-and-after can support useful reflection but cannot establish causation. The educator decides whether to continue, modify or stop the intervention. Necessary assistance stays available to learners even when that prevents a comparison. The Shared Growth Compact governs who can see or use growth evidence and supports non-penalization, auditability and private developmental work.

Quiet Intelligence explains the starting evidence. Formal evaluation operates on an institution-authorized, human-sealed record. Educator Growth uses evidence for objectives chosen by the educator under the Compact. Intervention measurement examines what followed an action. Institution-level improvement uses governed aggregates to choose and examine department or school actions, with individual records protected.

Exclusions include growth-based educator rankings, autonomous adaptive paths, a general workflow composer and user-assembled dashboards. [S1]
